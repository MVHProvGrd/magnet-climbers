What to build next

The three things that were queued here are built. There is a reason to open the app tomorrow now; what follows is about making a score worth caring about, and giving each update something to show.

The return loop is in: a daily climb everyone shares, three missions written against counters we already kept, and a streak that pays for turning up. That was the gap, and it is closed.

Next is worth caring about: a league where an ordinary player can be near the top, a door that changes every month, and the recorder that ghosts and races both need. Money and packaging wait for a few weeks of daily numbers.

Now in this order

The question has moved on from "why open this again" to "why does my score matter". These three answer that one.

1

Weekly league

Buckets of about thirty by lifetime metres; top promote, bottom drop.

With a daily board live, this is the next thing that makes a score worth caring about. The global board is unwinnable for everyone outside the top ten, so it stops motivating almost every player; a bucket is a board you can be near the top of. The metres are already banked per player, so the work is a grouping job and a tab.

sizelarge worker: a weekly bucketing jobboard tabtier badge
2

Themed fridge of the month

A different kitchen, a different door, one limited pattern.

The daily run is the thing worth clicking; a monthly door is the thing worth posting. The art pipeline is the strongest part of this project and the surfaces, seams and palette are already data, so this turns that strength into a reason to show up in a feed.

sizemedium scenery palettean art packone pattern behind a date
3

Input recorder

Log every fling and climb as {tick, id, vector}.

The gate in front of ghosts, async races and any real score validation — and now that a daily board exists, validation matters more than it did yesterday. Invisible to players on its own, which is exactly why it should be built deliberately rather than as step one of a race feature.

sizemedium game.ts input capturesnapshot formatworker: replay check

Then once the loop closes

These pay off once there is a reason to come back. In order, but the order is softer.

4

Ghost of your best run

Your own best climb, drawn on the door beside you.

Straight off the recorder, and the first thing it pays for. A line on the fridge says how far you got; a ghost says how you got there, which is the part worth beating.

sizemedium recorder playbacka second climber drawn ghosted
5

Async race from a share link

Race the run your friend actually climbed, not their number.

The share links already exist and already carry a height. Racing the recording turns a number into an opponent, and it needs no server beyond storing the tape.

sizelarge share linksworker: tape storagerace UI
6

Accounts

Sign in on top of the link codes.

Link codes carry a profile between devices today, which covers most of it. Accounts become worth the migration once there is a streak and a league standing worth losing — which, as of this week, there is.

Holding and why

Live ghost race

A Durable Object per match, inputs over a socket, the server settling the result. Real work, and it only makes sense after async racing proves anyone wants to race at all.

Capacitor, AdMob, IAP

Packaging and money. The loop now brings people back; give it a few weeks of daily numbers before wrapping it.

Offline income, prestige, season pass

The shape of a much bigger game. Revisit when the daily board says the core loop holds.

Decisions two settled, one open

Answered: crew is cut, not paused

The dead paths came out — crew rules, the lineup, SYNC, stacking, the human ladder, reserve climbers, the Expeditions tile and the four upgrades a lone climber could not feel. archive/crew holds the expedition recipes and a note on what went and what stayed; CREW_DESIGN.md is still the plan if it comes back one verb at a time.

Answered: the daily run is what gets promoted

So it was built first, and the streak was built to sit under it. The themed fridge is the thing that gives a post something to look at, which is why it is second on the list rather than first.

Open: how long does a daily board keep its rows?

Every day writes a row per player and nothing clears them. Fine for months; worth a retention rule before it is worth a bill. Say the word and the Worker drops days older than thirty on the next write.
